Why the framework doesn't deal with traits or causal latent variables. E.g., why do we say that a depression scale measures a mix of subjective experiences and self reported behaviors rather than the latent trait of depression?

-  These are not mutually exclusive. Traits and causal latent variables are simply not useful for the current goals of describing trends in measurement modes. This is in part due to the fact that, on close enough inspection, there is alwasys a seperateion between observed and latent variable. 
- The rationale of our approach can also be seen in the example of the trait of impulsivity and its many measures. Rather than taking a macro view of saying these attempt to measure a latent trait, it is useful for our pursposes to observe that individual measures, at a micro level, attempt to measure different things and in different ways, even if collectively one might argue they represent facets of the same latent trait of impuslivity (and indeed others would disagree, see REF 2017 paper on the poor association between measures)
  - self perceived trait of impulsivity is a self report of subjective experience
  - Go/No-Go task to measure inhibitory control is a behavioral measure of ability
  - the Risky, Impulsive, and Self-Destructive Behavior Questionnaire (RISQ) is a self-report about behavior because it asks about specific prior overt behaviors.
